# Scientific charter

## Claim boundary

OpenWorldGen v0.1 is a scenario-analysis instrument. Every result is
conditional on a named model, data vintage, parameter distribution, policy
definition, shock definition, and random-seed ensemble. A run is not evidence
that one future will occur.

The engine may earn a forecasting qualification only through preregistered,
out-of-sample comparisons with simpler baselines. Until then, UI, reports, and
documentation must use the words *scenario*, *conditional*, and *simulation*.

## Falsifiability

Every reference model must publish:

1. its accounting identities and invariants;
2. the agent observations, actions, and learning rules;
3. calibration and holdout targets;
4. parameters that are weakly identified;
5. sensitivity and ablation results;
6. the conditions under which the model fails;
7. scale-convergence evidence.

A failed invariant invalidates a run. A failed empirical gate limits the claim;
it must never be converted into a favorable narrative.

## Comparison, not monoculture

Agent-based results are compared with accounting models, microsimulation,
econometric baselines, and established macro models where available. Model
pluralism is a test, not an inconvenience.

## Human behavior and ethics

Human heterogeneity is represented through empirically motivated constraints,
habits, preferences, information, expectations, social influence, and
liquidity. IQ is not a rationality coefficient. Protected attributes are used
only when a documented research question and governance basis require them.

## Decision support

Policy results are distributions. The default comparison is a Pareto frontier
and minimax regret across inflation, output, employment, distributional
effects, defaults, and fiscal cost. A single recommendation requires explicit
objective weights, constraints, and risk tolerance supplied by the decision
owner.
